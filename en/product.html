01 / LIE

LIE · Inputs, results and checks, together

The following example from the mathematics edition illustrates inputs and results.

What you provide

Question: A has 18 books, 6 more than B. After A gives B 3 books, how many does each have?

What you receive

Task goals: determine the book counts for A and B after the transfer. The paired response lists two target objects.

How to check it

Match A, B and “how many does each have” to the question. Scope: task goals of this example question only.

What you provide

Paired response: B initially has 18-6=24 books; A now has 18-3=15; B now has 24+3=27.

What you receive

The paired result identifies 18-6=24 in the first step for review. The subtraction result should be 12.

How to check it

Locate the first-step quote and calculate 18 − 6 = 12 independently. The final answer is separately marked undetermined; no overall learner state is inferred.

What you provide

The two response-check records in the paired example result.

What you receive

Count this fixed response: 1 definite review finding; 1 undetermined finding; 2 total.

How to check it

Match the first-step expression and the final answer to their two records: 1 + 1 = 2. This summarises the example response; no statistics request was made and no overall learner state is implied.
